TEK830 Capstone Project 
Digital Innovation Prototype and Project Showcase Site 
Project Overview 
As part of this course, student teams will create a digital solution that addresses a 
practical challenge presented by an industry partner. The project combines innovation, 
sustainability, user-centered design, and technology development. During the course, 
teams will participate in workshops, complete project milestones, conduct interviews 
with relevant stakeholders, and receive feedback from instructors and peers. 
The ﬁnal project consists of two main outputs: 
1. An interactive software prototype. 
2. A project website that explains and demonstrates the solution. 
Selected projects may be recognized by external stakeholders based on their quality, 
originality, and potential value. 
 
1. Interactive Software Prototype 
Objective 
Design and build a software-based solution that helps address a meaningful 
organizational or operational challenge. The solution should demonstrate technical 
feasibility, user value, and consideration of sustainability impacts. 
Key Expectations 
Problem Relevance 
The proposed solution should address a clearly deﬁned challenge and provide a 
meaningful improvement or opportunity. 
Functional Prototype 
The prototype must include working functionality rather than only wireframes or visual 
mock-ups. Users should be able to interact with at least one core feature. 
Data Usage 
Use organizational, publicly available, simulated, or sample data where appropriate to 
demonstrate how the solution works. 
User Focus 
Interface design and functionality should reﬂect the needs of intended users.

Sustainability Considerations 
Teams should explain how the solution may contribute to environmental, social, or 
economic sustainability and, where possible, propose indicators for evaluating impact. 
Originality 
The project should demonstrate creativity through a new idea, a novel application of 
existing technologies, or a signiﬁcant improvement to current practices. 
Software Development 
A working implementation is required. Acceptable formats may include web 
applications, mobile applications, desktop software, dashboards, analytics tools, 
decision-support systems, or similar digital products. 
Demonstration 
The solution must be demonstrable through direct interaction, a live presentation, or a 
recorded walkthrough showing system responses to user actions. 
Authentic Development 
Teams should be able to describe and demonstrate their own contribution to the design 
and development process. 
Example Areas of Interest 
Potential project themes might include: 
• Energy efficiency and resource optimization 
• Workplace safety and risk management 
• Waste reduction and circular-economy initiatives 
• Data-driven decision support 
• Employee well-being and operational sustainability 
• Artiﬁcial intelligence applications in industry

2. Project Website 
Objective 
Create a website that communicates the project's purpose, development process, and 
outcomes to both technical and non-technical audiences. 
Content Requirements 
The website should: 
• Clearly explain the problem being addressed. 
• Present the proposed solution and its key beneﬁts. 
• Describe major prototype features and how users interact with them. 
• Use text, images, diagrams, videos, or other media to communicate the project 
effectively. 
Required Elements 
Presentation Materials 
Include ﬁnal presentation slides and a short project pitch video. 
The video should be understandable to a broad audience and may use any suitable 
format, such as: 
• Team presentation 
• Demonstration video 
• Animation 
• Story-based scenario 
• Slide-based narration 
Prototype Demonstration 
Provide either: 
• An embedded interactive version of the prototype, or 
• A video demonstration of the system in use. 
Project Information 
The site should also contain: 
• Project summary 
• Team member information

• Contact details 
• References and sources 
• A brief explanation of any generative AI tools used during the project 
Overall Deliverable 
Each team will submit: 
• A working software prototype demonstrating a digital innovation. 
• A companion website that communicates the solution, development process, 
and expected impact. 
Together, these deliverables should showcase technical development, innovation 
practices, user-centered design, and sustainability considerations. 
Evaluation Criteria 
Projects may be assessed using criteria such as: 
• Relevance and originality of the idea 
• Quality and usability of the prototype 
• Evidence of a user-centered design process 
• Consideration of sustainability impacts 
• Effectiveness of communication and presentation 
• Quality of the project website 
• Compliance with project requirements 
 
Recommendations for Success 
Communication 
• Present a coherent and compelling narrative. 
• Make the value of the solution immediately understandable. 
• Adapt communication to both technical and non-technical audiences. 
Project Scope 
• Focus on a speciﬁc problem rather than attempting to solve everything. 
• Prioritize a small number of well-developed features. 
• Emphasize functionality, clarity, and usability.

Research 
• Review existing solutions and industry practices. 
• Conduct interviews and gather stakeholder feedback. 
• Use benchmarking to identify opportunities for differentiation. 
Impact 
• Prioritize practical value and measurable outcomes. 
• Link design decisions to organizational and sustainability goals. 
Development Quality 
• Maintain clean, reliable, and maintainable code. 
• Seek feedback regularly and iterate based on insights. 
• Demonstrate thoughtful design choices throughout the project. 
 
More tips 
• Show what’s behind your idea. IKEA will repeatedly ask for more detail on how 
things actually work. Don’t just describe; demonstrate. 
• Clarify the user experience. Who uses your tool, how often, and for what 
decisions? 
• Show what’s real vs. conceptual. Be transparent about what your prototype does 
now and what’s planned for later. 
• Integrate IKEA’s feedback. Many of their questions will point to missing details — 
use those as a guide for what to strengthen. 
• If needed, run one or two short user tests and/or interviews to add credibility and 
reﬁne your design story. 
• Make your visuals central. Show your prototype early, and talk over it —  not the 
other way around.
