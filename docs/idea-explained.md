# Prisväxeln: the idea in plain English

## The problem

Emma is 32 and is planning a home for her young family. She wants to choose products with a lower environmental impact, but her household has a limited budget.

Imagine that Emma needs a storage cabinet. She compares two cabinets that meet the same practical needs. One has a lower modelled environmental footprint, but it costs more than she can afford. Emma may have to choose the less expensive option, even though she wanted to choose differently.

The challenge is also IKEA’s. A lower price could make a more considered choice affordable, but it could also lead to more people buying more products. The impact per product might go down while the total impact goes up.

## The idea

Prisväxeln explores how IKEA could make a comparable, lower-impact option more affordable when a customer is already planning to buy something.

### What Emma does

Emma enters her budget and compares two cabinets that meet the same storage needs. She sees the final price, the product differences and example environmental information in one place. In our example, a 150 kr offer brings the alternative down from 999 kr to 849 kr, within Emma’s 900 kr budget. She can choose it or keep her original choice. There is no pressure to buy.

### What IKEA does

An IKEA campaign or category team sets a product offer that is available on the same terms to customers. The team reviews example scenarios before deciding whether an offer is worth exploring. The scenarios show the effect of product switches, additional purchases, total modelled climate impact, virgin material and the campaign’s discount budget.

This matters because a lower impact per product does not guarantee a lower total impact. If the offer leads to enough additional purchases, the overall result can become worse. The example therefore shows both a scenario that stays within stated limits and one that exceeds them.

## Why Emma is the main persona

Emma is the person whose original problem starts the design: she wants a sustainable choice but cannot spend beyond her household budget. The IKEA team has a supporting role. It creates and evaluates the offer that could close the price gap. The customer view makes the benefit real for Emma; the IKEA view checks whether the offer could also work at a wider scale.

## What the prototype demonstrates

The prototype is a concept demonstration, not a live IKEA service. Its products, prices, environmental values, budgets and demand scenarios are fictional. Emma can change the example budget and select an option. The IKEA view lets visitors switch between two pre-set demand scenarios. These interactions explain the idea; they do not place orders, activate offers or connect to IKEA systems.

## In one sentence

Prisväxeln explores how IKEA could help Emma afford a comparable lower-impact product while checking that the offer does not increase total environmental impact as demand grows.
